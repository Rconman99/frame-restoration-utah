1. What should you do after hail in Midway?

Do not head outside just because the hail has stopped. Check current conditions and local alerts first. The National Weather Service’s after-storm guidance recommends staying informed because additional storms may follow, avoiding downed power lines, and staying out of damaged structures.

Keep your inspection to safe, accessible locations. You do not need ladder photographs to start a conversation with a roofer. If a branch, utility line, broken glass, or unstable material makes an area unsafe, leave that area alone. A photograph is not worth an injury. For an immediate threat to life or safety, use emergency services rather than an online roofing form.

2. What signs of possible roof damage can you check safely?

Begin with a short note on your phone. Record the property location, the date and approximate time you noticed the weather, and what looked different afterward. If the time is uncertain, say so. A clear observation is more useful than filling in a detail you cannot confirm.

  • Take a wide view first. Show which side of the home you are looking at before taking a closer photograph from the same safe spot.
  • Record visible debris. Photograph fallen roofing pieces where they landed. Do not climb up to find where they came from.
  • Note exterior changes. Include anything unusual on accessible gutters, downspouts, siding, windows, or outdoor equipment without assuming hail caused every mark.
  • Describe indoor observations. Tell the team if you see a new stain or water entering, and which room is affected. Do not enter an unsafe attic or touch wet electrical equipment.
  • Keep the originals. Save the original photo files along with your notes. A social-media screenshot is useful context, but it should not replace photographs of your own property.

3. Does hail damage mean your roof needs replacement?

The National Weather Service includes “hail that is an inch or larger” in its severe-thunderstorm definition. Hail measuring 1.0 inches or more meets that size criterion, but it is not a rule that every exposed roof must be replaced. We do not estimate a hailstone’s exact size from a hand-held photo without a reliable scale.

The National Roofing Contractors Association’s hail guidance describes inspection as the way to assess roof damage. Its examples include shingle penetration, fractures, and significant loss of surface granules. It also distinguishes cosmetic effects from damage that may require replacement of affected material.

When you speak with an inspector, ask them to separate what they observed from what they infer. “There is a mark here” and “this section needs repair for this reason” are different statements. A useful explanation should help you understand the recommendation, not pressure you to agree because another home on the street is having work done.

Midway hail and roof inspection questions

Does hail at my Midway home mean I need a new roof?

No. Hail at a property does not establish that its roof needs replacement. Ask for an inspection that explains the observed condition and why any repair is recommended.

What should I send when requesting an inspection?

Send your name, Midway property address, preferred contact method, approximate storm time if known, and a short description of what you noticed. Add photos taken from a safe location; do not climb onto the roof for pictures.

Can a photo of hail show how badly my roof was damaged?

No. A hail photograph provides context, not a roof diagnosis. A photo without a reliable scale also cannot establish an exact hail diameter. Roof condition must be assessed separately.

Do I need to wait for a ceiling leak before requesting an inspection?

No. You can request an inspection when you have a concern after hail, even without an indoor leak. Tell the roofer whether any water is entering so that the request can be assessed appropriately.
