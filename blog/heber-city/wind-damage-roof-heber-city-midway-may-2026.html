What the May 13 Storm Did to Roofs in the Heber Valley

Wind damage is often invisible from the ground. On Heber City and Midway jobs after the storm, here's what we found:

  • Missing and lifted shingles — High winds get under shingle edges and peel or rip them off. Once a shingle is lifted, the seal bond is broken and it won't lay flat again.
  • Damaged flashing — The metal around chimneys, vents, and roof edges is especially wind-vulnerable. Loose or bent flashing is one of the top water entry points on a roof.
  • Granule loss — Wind-driven debris scours the protective granules off your shingles, shortening their lifespan even when the shingles look intact from the ground.

The tricky part: wind damage is often invisible from the ground but shows up as a leak weeks or months later — after the next rainstorm or spring snowmelt.

Many policies address sudden wind damage, but the final answer depends on your policy language, cause of loss, deductible, exclusions, and carrier findings. Source-Backed Heber Wind Damage Checks

After a Heber Valley wind event, the best inspection plan ties visible damage to wind exposure, snow-load context, ventilation, and contractor verification instead of assuming every lifted shingle has the same cause.

  • 50-year ground snow load estimates should be checked by address or coordinates through Utah State University's tool when wind damage exposes older decking or framing questions. See the USU Utah Ground Snow Load Map.
  • 3,350 ft by 3,350 ft is the snow-load map grid size USU describes, so parcel-level checks matter in the Heber Valley.
  • 1/150 attic ventilation is the IRC baseline for vented attic or enclosed rafter space, with a 1/300 exception only when required conditions are met. See IRC R806.2 attic ventilation.
  • 32F-or-lower temperature records and snowfall records should be checked against National Weather Service Salt Lake data when freeze-thaw damage may be part of the roof condition. See the NWS Salt Lake City Climate Book.
  • License and liability insurance verification is recommended by Utah's Division of Consumer Protection before hiring a home-improvement contractor. See Utah DCP home-improvement guidance.
