How To Check If A Roofer Is Licensed In Utah: 2026 Guide for Utah Homeowners

Last updated: November 18, 2026

Written and reviewed by Landon Yokers, Owner of Frame Restoration Utah. Published under Frame Restoration Utah LLC; Utah DOPL contractor #14256097-5501.

QUICK ANSWER: To check a Utah roofer's license, search the company in the state's Licensee Lookup and Verification System before you sign anything. Utah issues contractor licenses to the business entity, not the salesperson, and business must be conducted in the name on the license, so the name on your contract should match the license record. Utah also requires contractors to carry liability insurance, so ask for a current certificate. Frame Restoration Utah offers free inspections at 435-292-8802.

Why the license check matters on a Utah roof

Utah's Department of Commerce puts the baseline simply: "Contractors in Utah are required to obtain a license and liability insurance." A roof replacement is a large, mostly invisible job. Once the shingles are on, you cannot see the deck repairs, the ice barrier at the eaves or the flashing details, so the company you hire matters as much as the materials it uses. A license check is the fastest way to confirm you are dealing with a business the state knows about, that carries insurance and that answers to the Division of Professional Licensing.

The check also protects you when a city inspector gets involved. Herriman's building FAQ, for example, notes that "Almost all contractors who do work that must be permitted must be licensed with the State of Utah." Cities along the Wasatch Front verify licenses when permits are pulled, so a contractor who cannot show one is also a contractor who may not be able to permit your job properly. Checking takes a few minutes and costs nothing.

Step one: look up the company, not the salesperson

The Division of Professional Licensing explains that "Licenses are issued to entities (corporations, LLC's, partnerships, joint ventures, etc.), not to the owner or qualifier of the entity." That detail matters when someone knocks on your door. The person in front of you may be a salesperson, a subcontractor or a referral partner. What you want to verify is the business that will sign your contract, pull the permit and stand behind the work.

Ask for the company's full legal name, then search it in the state's Licensee Lookup and Verification System, which the Department of Commerce links from its home improvement guidance. Look for an active status and a contractor classification that covers roofing work. If the lookup shows an expired, inactive or unrelated license, or you cannot find the company at all, stop and ask questions before going any further.

Step two: match the names on every document

DOPL states that "Business must be conducted in the name on the license." A company can operate under an assumed name, but DOPL says that name must be submitted to the division and owned by the licensee. In practice, the name on your estimate, your contract, the permit and the insurance certificate should all trace back to the same licensed business.

Mismatched names are one of the most common warning signs after a storm. A truck with one name, a contract with another and an insurance certificate with a third usually means the person selling the job is not the company that will do it. The state takes this seriously. DOPL warns that a contractor who "loans" a license and one who "borrows" it "are both guilty of class A misdemeanors." If the names do not line up, ask for an explanation in writing. Step three: confirm the insurance is current

DOPL's FAQ states: "Utah law requires that every active contractor license must maintain general liability insurance and financial responsibility." An active license tells you the requirement applies, but it does not tell you the policy is current today or that its limits suit your job. Ask the contractor to have its insurance agent send you a certificate of general liability insurance directly, and read the policy dates and the named insured.

Roofing is physical work at height, on someone else's property. If a ladder slips into a window or a crew member is hurt on your driveway, you want the contractor's coverage to respond, not yours. Ask about workers' compensation as well. A professional roofer handles these requests every week and will not be offended by them.

Step four: get it in writing and tie it to the permit

Once the license and insurance check out, get a written contract that names the licensed business, describes the scope, including tear-off, decking repairs, underlayment, ice barrier, flashing and ventilation, and states how the permit will be handled. Ask for the permit number before work begins, and keep the final inspection result with your contract. Our roof replacement quote checklist lists what a complete estimate should include.

Be cautious about large payments before materials are delivered or work begins, and keep receipts for every payment. Written terms, a permit and a licensed contractor give you a clear record if anything later needs to be fixed.

What to do if something does not add up

If a contractor cannot show a license, the names do not match, or the insurance certificate never arrives, the safest choice is to keep looking. If you already hired someone and run into problems, the Department of Commerce advises: "If you encounter such an issue and are not able to satisfactorily resolve it directly with the contractor, contact the Division of Consumer Protection." Licensing complaints go to the Division of Professional Licensing.

Most roofing problems start with a rushed decision, often in the days after a hail or wind storm when crews from out of state arrive. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if a roofer is licensed in Utah?

Search the company's full legal name in the state's Licensee Lookup and Verification System, linked from the Department of Commerce. Look for an active contractor license that covers roofing, and make sure the business name matches your contract.

Do roofers in Utah need a license?

Utah's Department of Commerce states that contractors in Utah are required to obtain a license and liability insurance. Cities also verify contractor licenses when permits are pulled for work that must be permitted.

Is a Utah contractor license issued to the person or the company?

DOPL issues contractor licenses to the business entity, such as a corporation or LLC, not to the owner or qualifier. Verify the company that will sign your contract, not just the salesperson at your door.

Does a Utah roofer need liability insurance?

Yes. DOPL states that every active contractor license must maintain general liability insurance and financial responsibility. Ask for a current certificate sent directly from the contractor's insurance agent.

Who do I contact about a problem with a Utah contractor?

If you cannot resolve the issue directly with the contractor, the Department of Commerce advises contacting the Division of Consumer Protection. Licensing complaints go to the Division of Professional Licensing.
