Salt Lake City's housing is old by Utah standards. Census ACS 2020–2024 estimates put the median year built at 1965, with about 78% of homes built before 2000 and roughly a quarter built in 1939 or earlier. Plenty of those roofs have already been patched, re-flashed, or covered with a second layer, and hail or wind goes straight for the weakest of those old repairs.

It is also a city of renters: fewer than half of occupied homes are owner-occupied, per the same ACS estimates. For a duplex in Liberty Wells or a converted house near the U, the storm scope is written so an owner or property manager who was not there can see each finding, slope by slope.

Four kinds of storm damage on Salt Lake City roofs

These are the patterns that show up again and again on Salt Lake City homes and small buildings after a storm.

Hail on Older Asphalt

Bruised and granule-stripped shingles on aging roofs, plus dented vents, gutters, and soft metals that show the hail's size and direction.

Wind-Lifted Shingles

Creased or missing tabs on ridges and rakes, common on exposed east bench homes and older roofs with tired sealant strips.

Low-Slope Additions

Ponding, torn seams, and failed flashing on the flat rear additions many older Salt Lake City homes carry.

Storm-Opened Roofs

Emergency tarping when a limb, gust, or hail leaves the roof open, then a permanent repair. See emergency roof tarping.

How a Salt Lake City storm repair runs

A Salt Lake City storm repair moves in four stages, and the first one only happens if the roof is open.

  1. 1. Stop the water

    If the roof is open, the crew tarps it and fastens the tarp to hold until the permanent repair, as weather and safety allow. Wet ceilings and attics get photographed now, before they dry and the evidence fades.

  2. 2. The inspection

    A walk around the house first, looking at gutters, downspouts, vents, and window screens for dents. Then the roof itself, slope by slope: hail hits marked and counted, lifted tabs photographed, and old patches separated from new damage.

  3. 3. The written scope

    You get the photos, the findings for each slope, and a plain recommendation: repair, or replace. In a historic district, the scope also records the existing material and color so the city review question can be answered first.

  4. 4. Repair day

    Old material comes off down to the decking, and any soft or rotted boards are replaced before new underlayment, flashing, and shingles go on. The low-slope addition, if there is one, is done as part of the same job.

How big has the hail been near Salt Lake City this year?

Large hail came close to Salt Lake City in 2026. Preliminary Storm Prediction Center reports for the evening of June 25, 2026 list hail up to 1.75 inches in Taylorsville and about 1.5 inches in Holladay, both a short drive from the city's neighborhoods.

Hail does not have to be golf-ball sized to hurt a Salt Lake City roof. On 30- and 40-year-old shingles, smaller stones can still crack the mat or knock off the granules that protect it. Frame Restoration Utah separates fresh hail bruising from plain old age before recommending anything, because a roof that is simply worn out needs a different conversation than one a storm just damaged.

Neighborhoods that need a closer look after a storm

Salt Lake City storm inspections take the most care where the roofs are oldest and most complicated. In the Avenues and on Capitol Hill, steep pitches, dormers, and old chimneys create flashing joints that a storm can pry open. In Sugar House, Liberty Wells, and 9th and 9th, bungalows often have a low-slope rear addition tied into a steeper main roof. On the east bench, more exposure means more wind on ridges and rakes. In Rose Park and Glendale, mid-century ramblers sometimes hide decking damage under an old overlay.

Yalecrest and the city's other local historic districts can add a review step if a repair changes roofing material or visible details. We record what is on the house now and point owners to Salt Lake City Historic Preservation so that question is settled before work starts.

Worth gathering before the inspection

None of this is required, but each item makes a Salt Lake City storm inspection faster and the written scope sharper.

  • The date and approximate time of the storm.
  • Photos from the ground of each side of the home, taken before anything is moved.
  • Any interior leak spots, with photos of ceilings or attic stains.
  • The roof's approximate age and any past repairs you know about.
  • Whether the home is in a local historic district (for example, Yalecrest or the Avenues).
  • If you are a landlord or property manager, the best contact for tenant access.

Paperwork for your insurer

A Salt Lake City storm inspection ends with dated photos, labeled findings, and a written repair scope you can share with your insurer. Coverage decisions stay between you and your insurance company; we document storm damage and repair roofs, we do not adjust claims, and required out-of-pocket amounts remain the homeowner's responsibility under Utah law.
