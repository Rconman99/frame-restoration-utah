What we repair on Salt Lake City roofs

Most repair calls in Salt Lake City come from a handful of places: flashing around old chimneys and dormers, lifted or missing tabs on ridges and rakes, worn pipe boots and vents, and the seams of low-slope rear additions that many older homes carry.

Salt Lake City's housing is old by Utah standards. Census ACS 2020–2024 estimates put the median year built at 1965, so many roofs have already been patched more than once. A good repair accounts for the earlier patches instead of covering them.

Repair or replace?

Not every leak means a new roof. If the damage is local and the rest of the roof has life left, a repair is the right answer and the written scope will say so. When the shingles are brittle across several slopes or the decking is soft, we explain why a replacement is the better spend. See roof replacement in Salt Lake City.

Neighborhood notes

In the Avenues and on Capitol Hill, steep pitches, dormers, and chimneys create flashing joints that fail first. In Sugar House, Liberty Wells, and Yalecrest, older bungalows often carry layered repairs and flat rear additions. In local historic districts, a repair that changes material or visible details may need city review; we record existing materials and point you to Salt Lake City Historic Preservation.

Roof Repair questions in Salt Lake City

How do I know if my Salt Lake City roof needs a repair or a replacement?
We inspect each slope and photograph what we find. If damage is local and the rest of the roof has life left, the scope recommends a repair. If shingles are brittle across several slopes or the decking is soft, we explain why a replacement may be the better choice.
Can you repair a flat or low-slope addition?
Yes. We inspect the membrane, seams, and flashing on the addition along with the main roof and include both in one written scope.
Do historic district rules apply to roof repairs in Salt Lake City?
They can. If a repair in a local historic district changes roofing material or visible details, the city may need to review it. We record the existing materials and point you to Salt Lake City Historic Preservation before work starts.
