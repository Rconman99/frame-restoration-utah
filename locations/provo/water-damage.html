Provo is a renter’s city. Census ACS 2020–2024 estimates count only about four in ten occupied homes as owner-occupied, which fits a town built around Brigham Young University. Near campus, older single-family houses have been divided into a main floor and a basement unit, and Provo’s accessory dwelling unit rules allow one unit, often in the basement, on an owner-occupied home in approved areas.

The houses themselves span decades. The same estimates put the median year built at 1983, with almost half of Provo homes built before 1980 and nearly four in five before 2000. So a water call in Provo is often a 1970s or 1980s house with two households stacked on top of each other, where a leak upstairs turns into a ceiling, a wall, and a carpet problem downstairs.

Four ways water gets into a Provo home

Most Provo water damage starts in one of these places.

Upstairs leaks

Supply lines, toilets, and tubs on the main floor that leak down into a finished basement or basement unit.

Water heaters and laundry

Tanks and washers in shared basement utility rooms, often a wall away from a bedroom.

Frozen pipes

Cold-snap splits in exterior walls and garages, worse in a house left empty over a school break.

Roof leaks

Water through a ceiling after wind or hail. We are roofers too, so the roof repair can go on the same scope.

What happens after you call

A Provo water job runs in four stages, and each one is explained to whoever is living there.

  1. 1. The first hour

    The water source is confirmed off, and wet light fixtures and outlets are flagged so nobody uses them. Both units are walked, because water from upstairs rarely stops at one room. Then extraction pulls water out of the carpet and off the floor.

  2. 2. The first day

    Meters on ceilings, walls, and floors in both units, with every reading marked. Wet ceiling drywall and insulation come down where the readings say so, and drywall that wicked water from the floor is cut at a measured line.

  3. 3. Drying

    Dehumidifiers and air movers run in each affected space. If a tenant is still living there, the equipment is placed so the rest of the unit stays usable where that is safe, and return visits check the same marked points until the numbers say dry.

  4. 4. Repair

    Ceiling, drywall, insulation, flooring, trim, and paint, written as a rebuild scope separate from the emergency work, so the owner can see each line and the tenant knows what to expect.

Who should call when a Provo rental has water damage?

Whoever finds the water should stop it and call. For water damage restoration in Provo, that is often a tenant at 2 a.m., and the owner hears about it second. Shut off the water, take photos, and call 435-292-8802; Provo water and flood emergencies are answered 24/7. Then tell the owner or property manager right away.

Owners, the documentation is written so you can follow it from out of town. Photos, readings, and the scope show what was wet, what came out, and what it will take to put back, with the emergency work kept separate from the rebuild. If the basement is a licensed accessory unit, ask Provo City about permits before the rebuild starts; the city requires a building permit when a basement is refinished to create an ADU.

Why do the benches and the river matter for water in Provo?

Provo’s east side climbs onto benches below Y Mountain and Rock Canyon. Those benches are old shorelines of Lake Bonneville, and homes built on them sit on sloped lots where spring melt and summer storms run downhill toward foundations and window wells. Lower down, the Provo River leaves Provo Canyon and crosses the north end of the city on its way to Utah Lake.

Most household water damage starts inside the house, but if you live near the river or on a steep bench lot, look the address up on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. For water damage restoration in Provo where the water came in from outside, see flooded basement cleanup.

Winter breaks and empty houses

Provo empties out between semesters. A house or unit left with the heat turned low over Christmas break is the classic setup for a frozen line that splits, thaws, and runs for days before anyone comes back. After a break, the drying job is usually bigger than a same-day leak because the water had time to travel.

If you are leaving for a break, keep the heat on, open cabinet doors on outside walls, and ask someone to check the place. If you come back to water, shut it off, photograph everything, and call.

What to have ready

Tenants and owners can each help the Provo water damage scope start right.

  • Water off at the main or the fixture.
  • Photos of every wet room in every unit, before anything moves.
  • When the leak started or was found.
  • The owner or property manager told, with their contact details handy.
  • Electronics, papers, and textbooks moved somewhere dry.
  • Insurance details for the owner and, if they have it, renter’s coverage for the tenant.

What the owner receives

A Provo water job ends with photos, moisture readings, and a written scope that separates emergency mitigation from permanent repairs, so the owner can share it with their insurer and the tenant can see what was done. Coverage decisions stay between the policyholders and their insurance companies; we document and restore, we do not adjust claims. If water sat long enough for mold to start, see mold remediation.
