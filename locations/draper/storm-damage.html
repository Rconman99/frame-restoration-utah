Draper's geography stacks three wind regimes into one zip code. The Point of the Mountain gap funnels pressure-driven flow between two valleys — the same reliable lift that makes the Flight Park world-class makes storm gusts hit Draper's western benches broadside. Corner Canyon adds downslope acceleration on the east side. And SunCrest, riding the Traverse Ridge a thousand feet above everyone else, takes mountain weather in a suburban package: deeper snow, harder freeze-thaw, fog ice, and the strongest sustained winds in the city.

The housing stock adds its own twist. Draper built fast over the last 25 years, and a lot of production roofs went on at minimum code — fine on a calm valley floor, underbuilt for gap-wind exposure. The most common storm call we get here is a newer two-story home with creased field shingles and a lifted ridge cap after a gusty front, with the owner surprised a "new" roof could be damaged at all.

Draper Storm Damage FAQs

Why is wind such a problem for Draper roofs? +
Draper sits against the Point of the Mountain — the gap between the Salt Lake and Utah Valley airsheds that's famous enough for wind that paragliders launch there year-round. Pressure differences between the two valleys drive accelerated flow through the gap, and Draper's bench neighborhoods catch it broadside. Add Corner Canyon's downslope gusts on the east side and SunCrest's exposed ridge at over 5,000 feet, and you get three distinct wind regimes in one city — all of them hard on shingles.
Does SunCrest really get worse weather than the rest of Draper? +
Yes — measurably. SunCrest rides the Traverse Ridge at roughly 1,000 feet above the valley floor, which means more snow, longer freeze-thaw cycling, more fog-driven ice, and the strongest sustained winds in Draper. Roofs up there age faster and fail differently: ice dams at the eaves, wind-creased field shingles, and fastener back-out on the gable ends. We inspect SunCrest roofs with mountain standards because that's effectively what they are.
Will insurance cover wind damage repair in Draper? +
Sudden wind, hail, and falling-tree damage is covered by most homeowner policies. Frame Restoration Utah documents the damage with photographs and a detailed line-item scope your adjuster can read, and meets your adjuster on the roof — the claim stays between you and your insurer; we document the damage, we don't adjust. Utah law keeps your deductible yours to pay; anyone offering to waive it is breaking the law and signaling how they cut corners.
My Draper home is newer — can it still have storm damage? +
Absolutely. Much of Draper was built in the last 25 years, and newer doesn't mean wind-proof — production builders often installed to minimum code, not to the gap-wind exposure the Point delivers. We routinely find creased shingles and lifted ridge caps on roofs under 15 years old, especially on the taller two-story homes that catch unobstructed wind. The good news: caught early, those are targeted repairs, not replacements.
