After a Windstorm Hits Draper

  • Start from a safe location. Follow active NWS warnings and do not inspect outdoors while lightning, hail, or damaging wind is present.
  • Photograph the ground and exterior. Record displaced materials, damaged gutters, fallen limbs, and roof edges from the ground; do not climb onto a wet or uncertain roof.
  • Check the interior. Photograph ceiling stains, attic drips, or water near penetrations and note when each condition was first observed.

A Scope Based on the Roof, Not the Weather Alert

A weather report establishes context, not a repair scope. The recommendation should connect each proposed item to a documented condition and identify the affected slope or component. For a larger scope, the contractor should explain how roof covering, underlayment, flashing, ventilation, and deck conditions affect the work, then confirm the applicable Draper permit requirements and manufacturer instructions.

What a Draper Roof-Condition Report Should Include

A useful report starts with the property address, inspection date, roof-covering type, approximate roof age if known, and a diagram or clear slope labels. Overview photographs establish orientation; close-ups should show the condition in context rather than only a tight image of a mark. The report should also identify ridge and hip caps, roof edges, flashings, vents, pipe boots, valleys, gutters, and any accessible interior evidence related to water entry.

The repair recommendation should separate immediate safety or temporary-protection needs from permanent work. It should state what can be repaired, what requires further evaluation, whether matching materials are available, and what was not accessible during the inspection. If a claim is involved, the homeowner can share that factual construction record with the carrier while coverage and settlement decisions remain with the carrier.

Draper Storm Damage FAQs

Why is wind such a problem for Draper roofs? +
Draper's position near Point of the Mountain and its range of neighborhood elevations can create different wind exposure from one property to another. Roof height, slope direction, edge condition, surrounding terrain, and the actual storm path all matter. A roof inspection should document displaced, creased, or missing materials at the property rather than infer damage from a weather alert alone.
Does SunCrest really get worse weather than the rest of Draper? +
SunCrest sits above Draper's valley-floor neighborhoods, so exposure can differ by elevation, slope, and nearby terrain. Elevation alone does not prove roof damage. An inspection should document wind-facing slopes, eaves, flashing, ventilation, snow or ice conditions, and the actual condition of the roofing materials.
