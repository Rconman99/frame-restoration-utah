Holladay sits tight against the Wasatch where Neffs Canyon and the Mt. Olympus drainages spill east-bench winds straight across town. The same tree canopy that makes Holladay one of the valley's most loved neighborhoods becomes its biggest roofing liability in a storm: heavy hardwood limbs over fifty-year-old rooflines. The September 2020 east-wind event made the math vivid — thousands of trees down across the east bench, and roofs from Olympus Cove to Holladay Boulevard punctured, crushed, or stripped.

Storm work here is two trades in one: careful structural repair where limbs broke decking, and wind-uplift repair where gusts worked shingles loose along ridgelines and west-facing slopes. Both fail quietly if patched cosmetically — which is why our inspections check the deck, not just the shingles.

After the Storm: A Holladay Homeowner's Checklist

  • If a limb hit the roof, clear the room below it. Deck punctures are structural; ceilings under them can sag or fail.
  • Photograph everything from the ground — downed limbs, displaced shingles, dented gutters, granules washed to the downspouts.
  • Call 435-292-8802 for 24/7 tarping. A properly anchored tarp is the difference between a roof repair and a roof-plus-interior repair.
  • Book the free inspection — east-wind uplift breaks shingle seals invisibly; the damage shows up two storms later as a leak.
  • Vet the door knockers. Crews canvass Holladay after every wind event — our Local Roofer vs Storm Chaser guide shows exactly what credentials to demand.

Holladay Storm Damage FAQs

What kind of storm damage is most common on Holladay roofs? +
Tree strikes lead the list. Holladay's mature canopy — the towering cottonwoods, spruces, and sycamores that define streets like Cottonwood Lane and the Olympus Cove foothills — sheds heavy limbs in east-wind events and wet spring snows. The September 2020 east-wind event toppled thousands of trees across the Salt Lake Valley's east bench, and Holladay's older neighborhoods were among the hardest hit. Wind uplift along Mt. Olympus's base and summer hail bruising round out the big three.
A tree limb just went through my Holladay roof — what do I do first? +
Get everyone out of the room below the strike, then call Frame Restoration Utah at 435-292-8802. A punctured deck is a structural issue, not just a leak — don't let anyone climb up to "throw a tarp on it." Our crew handles 24/7 emergency tarping with proper fall protection, stabilizes the opening, and returns for permanent deck and shingle repair once the roof is safe to fully assess.
Does insurance cover east-wind and tree damage in Holladay? +
Sudden wind and falling-tree damage is covered by most homeowner policies. Frame Restoration Utah photographs the damage, prepares a detailed line-item scope your adjuster can read, and meets your adjuster on site — the claim stays between you and your insurer; we document the damage, we don't adjust. Utah law makes your deductible yours to pay, so treat any "free roof, we'll eat the deductible" pitch as the red flag it is.
Are Holladay's older roofs more vulnerable to storm damage? +
Often, yes. Much of Holladay was built mid-century, and homes that haven't been reroofed recently may have brittle three-tab shingles, aging flashing, and decking that's absorbed decades of freeze-thaw cycles at the bench's elevation. Storms find those weak points first. A free inspection tells you whether a targeted repair will hold or whether the storm exposed a roof that was already at end of life.
