How an ice dam turns into a ceiling stain in Heber City

An ice dam leak in Heber City starts on a sunny afternoon after a big storm. Heat from the house melts the snow on the upper roof, the water runs down to the colder eave, and it freezes again into a ridge of ice. The next round of meltwater has nowhere to go but backward, up under the shingles and into the attic. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the insulation above it may have been wet for days.

Water damage restoration after an ice dam has two halves. Inside, we dry the attic, ceiling, and wall cavities and replace what cannot be saved. Outside, we repair the eave, valley, or flashing and look at whether the roof has the ice barrier and ventilation it needs so it does not happen again next February.

Spring runoff and the Heber Valley basement

Basement water damage in Heber City peaks when the snow comes off the mountains. The Provo River runs the length of the valley between Jordanelle and Deer Creek reservoirs, and in late spring the ground around many foundations stays saturated for weeks. Water finds the window well with no cover, the crack in an old foundation wall, or the sump pit whose pump quietly burned out over the winter. Testing the sump pump and clearing window wells before melt season is a small job that prevents a lot of wet carpet.

What the crew does on a Heber City water call

  1. 1. On arrival

    Stop the water, check for anything electrical near it, and photograph each wet room as found. Then the extraction hoses come in and the standing water goes out.

  2. 2. Before the crew leaves on day one

    Every wet wall, floor, and cabinet base gets a meter reading and a mark. Wet carpet pad and drywall that wicked water are cut out at a measured line, and dehumidifiers and air movers are set for the space. In a cold house, warmer air dries faster, so we may ask to turn the thermostat up.

  3. 3. Drying

    Return visits read the same marked points and move the equipment as areas dry. Thick framing and cold basements can take longer, and the readings, not the calendar, decide when drying is done.

  4. 4. Repairs inside and out

    With the structure dry, the written scope covers the rebuild: insulation, drywall, flooring, trim, and the roof, eave, or flashing that let the water in.

Why do Wasatch Back homes get so much winter water damage? +
