Heber City sits at more than 5,000 feet in a mountain valley, and its roofs live through a different storm year than the ones in Salt Lake. Summer thunderstorms bring hail and sudden outflow wind across the open valley floor. Winter brings long cold spells, heavy snow, and ice dams that push meltwater under shingles at the eaves.

The town is growing fast, too. Census ACS 2020–2024 estimates put the median year built at 2003, with about 42% of homes built before 2000, so new subdivisions at the edge of town sit alongside older houses near Main Street and in the original town grid. We work on both from our office at 142 S Main St.

Four ways a mountain storm shows up on the roof

Heber Valley storm damage usually takes one of these forms.

Summer Hail

Bruised shingles, cracked vents, and dented metal after valley thunderstorms, documented slope by slope.

Outflow and Canyon Wind

Lifted ridge caps, torn tabs, and loose metal trim on homes exposed across the open valley floor.

Snow Load and Ice Dams

Leaks at eaves, valleys, and flashing where ice dams back meltwater under the roof.

How a Heber Valley storm repair runs

A Heber City storm repair follows four stages, and the season decides how fast the last one can happen.

  1. 1. Stop the water

    If the roof is open, a tarp goes on and is fastened to hold through the next storm, as weather and safety allow. In winter, that can mean working around snow on the roof.

  2. 2. Inspect before the snow covers it

    Each slope is checked and photographed, metal ridge caps and trim included. After a fall hail storm, getting this done before the first big snow matters, because snow hides damage until spring.

  3. 3. Scope the whole roof system

    The written scope covers the storm damage and whether the eaves and valleys have the ice barrier and ventilation they need, because the next leak in Heber is often an ice dam.

  4. 4. Permanent repair when conditions allow

    Shingles, metal, and flashing are repaired or replaced when temperatures and roof conditions make it safe and sound to do the work.

If you are in Heber City, Midway, or elsewhere in the Heber Valley and saw hail that evening, a documented inspection before snow covers the roof is the best way to find damage. Snow on a damaged roof can turn a small bruise or lifted tab into a winter leak.

Why is winter storm damage different in Heber City?

Winter storm damage is different in Heber City because snow stays on roofs longer and ice dams form more often than in the Salt Lake Valley. When heat from the house melts snow on the upper roof, the water refreezes at the colder eaves and backs up under shingles. Heber City roofs need intact ice barrier at the eaves and valleys, good attic ventilation, and solid flashing to handle that.

Frame Restoration Utah documents ice dam leaks at the roof and inside, repairs the roof and flashing where the water got in, and can restore the interior damage under the same scope. If water has already reached ceilings or walls, see water damage restoration in Heber City.

Storm damage in Heber City is an emergency when the roof is open, a section of roofing or trim has blown off, or water is coming inside. What should Heber Valley second-home owners check after a storm?

Many Heber Valley homes sit empty for weeks at a time, so storm damage can go unnoticed until the next visit. After a hail storm, high wind, or a heavy snow, Heber City and Heber Valley owners who are away should have someone check for missing shingles or ridge caps, bent gutters or metal trim, and new ceiling stains, and should look at the home before snow hides the roof.

Frame Restoration Utah can inspect an unoccupied Heber Valley home with the owner's permission, send dated photos of each slope and any interior stains, and tarp an open roof right away so water does not sit inside while you are out of town. Helpful to gather before the inspection

Any of these make a Heber City storm inspection quicker.

  • The storm date and time (for example, the evening of September 17, 2026).
  • Ground-level photos of each side of the home, including any metal roofing or trim.
  • Photos of icicles or ice buildup at the eaves, if it is winter.
  • Any interior leak spots or ceiling stains.
  • The roof's approximate age and material.
  • If the home is a second home, the best contact for access.
