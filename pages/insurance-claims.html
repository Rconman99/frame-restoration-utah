What a Utah roofer can — and can't — do in a carrier-review file

Almost every roofer in Utah says they "help with insurance." Utah law draws a clear line between two activities:

  • Documenting roof damage and preparing a written scope — what a licensed Utah roofing contractor like Frame does: photos, drone imagery where useful, storm-event records, measurements, material notes, and a line-item construction estimate your carrier can review.
  • Adjusting the coverage decision itself — investigating, advocating on coverage, and settling a carrier file. Under Utah Code §31A-26-201, that work is reserved for licensed adjusters. A roofer who advocates on coverage or ties pricing to policy proceeds is on the wrong side of that line.

How the Storm-Damage Documentation Process Works

If your roof has been damaged by hail, wind, snow, or fallen debris, carrier review may apply under your homeowner policy. The construction documentation process is separate from the policy decision. Here is the safe sequence:

  • You document the storm event with date, photos, and visible damage notes
  • A licensed roofer inspects the roof and records roof-plane conditions, accessories, measurements, and material damage
  • You decide whether to request carrier review after reading your policy and considering the construction scope
  • Your carrier performs its review if you request it and makes policy decisions under your policy terms
  • Additional construction documentation can be provided if the roof scope needs clarification
  • Construction starts after the homeowner approves the final roofing scope, material selection, and schedule

Timelines vary by carrier, policy, weather volume, material availability, permit requirements, and homeowner decisions. Frame Restoration Utah keeps the roof documentation organized so the construction record is clear.

Common Claim Challenges

Carrier paperwork and construction scopes do not always use the same language. Here are common documentation challenges Utah homeowners face during the roofing process:

  • Waiting too long to document: Policy notice windows vary. Missing photos, missing storm dates, and delayed inspection notes make carrier review harder
  • Accepting a scope you do not understand: A carrier estimate may not match the construction reality. A contractor can review line items and document roof conditions for carrier review
  • Not having roof evidence ready: If no one has measured, photographed, and written down the roof conditions before carrier review, important construction details can be missed
  • Material and code references: Product grade, ventilation, ice-barrier, deck, and flashing details should be written down when they affect the construction scope
  • Policy-cost assumptions: Required out-of-pocket amounts, depreciation, and payment timing are policy questions; Frame documents construction details only

Carrier Review vs. Direct Repair

Not every roof issue needs carrier review. A few missing shingles from normal wear, a small leak around a pipe boot, or a minor flashing repair may be a direct construction repair.

If your roof has been hit by a documented storm event or the damage appears widespread, review your policy and decide whether to request carrier review.
