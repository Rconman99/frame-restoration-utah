Financing on Roofing and Restoration Projects

Frame Restoration Utah has partnered with Wisetack to offer financing on all roofing and restoration projects. Wisetack is the third-party financing provider; we are the licensed Utah roofing contractor doing the work. You can get pre-qualified in minutes with no impact to your credit score, which means you can look at your options before you have committed to anything at all.

This page exists for one reason: a roof rarely breaks at a convenient moment. A February ice dam or an August hail storm does not wait for a good month financially. Knowing a payment path exists lets you make the roofing decision on the roof's timeline instead of putting off work that gets more expensive the longer water moves through a deck.

You will not find roofing prices on this page or anywhere else on this site. There is a reason for that, and it is not evasiveness: a roof scope depends on pitch, square footage, layer count, deck condition, ventilation, flashing detail, access, and material choice. A number published before anyone has looked at your roof is a guess, and guesses in this trade are usually wrong in the direction that is bad for the homeowner. We inspect first, then put a real scope in writing.

How It Works, In Order

  1. Free inspection. We come out, get on the roof where it is safe to do so, and document condition with photos — shingle field, flashing, valleys, penetrations, eaves, ventilation, and the attic side where accessible.
  2. Written scope. You get a line-item scope of what the roof needs, what is optional, and what can reasonably wait a season.
  3. You choose how to pay. Out of pocket, or pre-qualify with Wisetack and see what terms you are offered. Nothing about the inspection obligates you to either one.
  4. Scheduling. Work is scheduled around weather. In the high valleys and at Park City elevation, that window is genuinely narrow, which is the main reason we would rather talk in September than in January.

Why Utah Roofs Do Not Reward Waiting

Freeze-thaw cycling is the mechanism that turns a small roofing problem into a large one in this state. Water gets under a lifted shingle or through a tired flashing joint, freezes overnight, expands, and opens the gap a little wider. Repeat that several dozen times between November and April and a repair becomes a deck replacement. In Heber City and the rest of the Heber Valley, the valley's bowl shape traps cold air inversions that extend those cycles well into April. Park City, at roughly 7,000 feet, sees annual snowfall that routinely exceeds 300 inches and temperature swings that stress roofing materials to their limits.

None of that is an argument for spending money you do not have. It is an argument for finding out what your roof actually needs, in writing, while the weather still allows choices.
