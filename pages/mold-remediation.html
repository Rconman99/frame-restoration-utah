You notice it first with your nose: a musty, basement smell in a room that should not have one. Then a gray shadow behind the toilet, or a dark bloom on the attic sheathing when you go up for the holiday boxes. Mold in a Utah home almost always traces back to water that stayed somewhere it should not, and the remediation that lasts is the one that deals with the water as well as the growth.

How remediation runs, step by step

Mold remediation from Frame Restoration Utah in Salt Lake City and northern Utah follows four steps, in order.

Find the Moisture Source

Moisture readings and inspection of the affected area to find the leak, intrusion, or condensation feeding the growth.

Fix the Source

Plumbing, roof, ventilation, or drainage repairs so the area can stay dry. Roof and attic repairs are handled in-house.

Contain and Remove

The work area is closed off to limit spread, and affected porous materials such as drywall and insulation are removed.

HEPA Clean and Dry

Remaining surfaces are HEPA vacuumed and cleaned, and the area is dried and checked before rebuild.

  1. 1. The inspection

    We follow the moisture, not just the stain. Meter readings around the growth show how far the water reached, which is usually farther than the visible spots.

  2. 2. Containment day

    Plastic sheeting goes up around the work area so spores do not travel through the rest of the house, and the affected drywall, insulation, or trim comes out.

  3. 3. Cleaning and drying

    Framing and surfaces left in place are HEPA vacuumed and cleaned, then dried and checked with the meter before anything is closed up.

  4. 4. Closing it up

    New insulation, drywall, and finishes go in only after the source is fixed and the readings are dry.

Where mold hides in Utah homes

In Utah homes, mold most often grows in four places, all of them somewhere water lingered:

  • Attics: roof leaks, ice dams, and bathroom fans vented into the attic instead of outside. See roof repair.
  • Basements: after floods, snowmelt seepage, or a failed sump pump. See flood cleanup.
  • Behind walls and under cabinets: slow plumbing and appliance leaks. See water damage restoration.
  • Around windows and exterior walls: winter condensation and water getting past siding or flashing. See siding replacement and repair.

Why bleach and paint do not work

Spraying a product on visible mold, or painting over it, does not remove the growth from a Utah home and does not keep it from coming back. Neither does running a dehumidifier while the leak keeps going. We would rather show you the source and fix it than sell you a treatment that only covers the symptom. Frame Restoration Utah documents what it finds with photos, moisture readings, and a written scope you can share with your insurer. Mold questions from Utah homeowners

What causes mold in Utah homes if the climate is dry?

Moisture from inside the house. Slow plumbing leaks, roof leaks and ice dams, flooded basements, bathroom fans that vent into the attic, and winter condensation on cold walls all keep materials wet. Dry outdoor air does not help a wall cavity that never dries.

Do you test for mold?

We focus on finding the moisture source and how far the growth has spread, using moisture readings and a visual inspection of the affected areas. If you need independent laboratory testing, for example before a sale, an independent tester can provide it.

Why fix the moisture source first?

Because mold comes back when the material stays wet. Removing growth without stopping the leak, the condensation, or the water intrusion only resets the clock.

Is mold remediation available 24/7?

No. Mold remediation is scheduled during business hours (Mon–Fri 8am–7pm, Sat 8am–6pm, Sun closed). If active water is causing the problem, call our 24/7 water emergency line at 435-292-8802.

Can you fix a roof leak that caused attic mold?

Yes. We are a licensed roofing contractor, so the roof leak or the ventilation problem behind attic mold and the remediation of the affected materials can be handled by the same team.
