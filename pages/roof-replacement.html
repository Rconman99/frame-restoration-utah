When to Replace Your Roof

Not every roof problem calls for a full replacement, but there are clear signs that patching and repairs will not cut it anymore. If your roof is 20 to 25 years old, has widespread storm damage across multiple slopes, or you are dealing with recurring leaks in different areas, it is likely time for a full replacement.

Other warning signs include:

  • Shingles that are curling, cracking, or losing granules across the entire roof
  • Sagging or soft spots in the roof deck
  • Daylight visible through the attic or widespread water staining on interior ceilings
  • Multiple previous patch repairs that keep failing
  • A documented inspection shows repair is no longer practical after storm, age, ventilation, or decking review

storm-related replacements

Many Utah roof replacements begin after hail, wind, or falling-debris damage, but carrier review is policy-specific. Your policy terms, cause of loss, exclusions, roof condition, and carrier findings determine whether repair, replacement, or no covered work applies.

Frame Restoration Utah specializes in storm-related replacements. We document visible roof conditions with photos, measurements, and a written construction scope your carrier can review. Coverage decisions stay between you and your insurer; our job is to make the roof evidence and replacement scope clear before work begins.

Replacement Readiness Checks We Verify

A Utah reroof is not just a shingle count. Before replacement, we check the roof assembly details that determine whether the new system will survive snow, sun, wind, and freeze-thaw cycles.

  • Ventilation: IRC R806.2 ventilation guidance helps confirm whether the attic needs more intake, exhaust, or baffles before new shingles go on.
  • Ice barriers: IRC R905.1.2 ice-barrier guidance informs eave and valley protection, especially in Heber, Park City, shaded Salt Lake neighborhoods, and other ice-dam-prone areas.
  • Snow load: The Utah Ground Snow Load Map helps frame reroof details for mountain and bench communities.
  • Material evidence: Class 4 impact-resistant shingle guidance supports material discussions, but any carrier discount must be verified directly with the carrier.
  • Contractor verification: Homeowners can check Utah DOPL licensing, Utah Commerce guidance, and BBB profile information before approving a reroof.
