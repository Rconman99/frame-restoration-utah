Flood Cleanup — Quick Facts

Sewage backups are outside our scope. Licensed Utah contractor (DOPL #14256097-5501).

Basement floods in Utah tend to follow the seasons. In spring, snowmelt and runoff saturate the ground around foundations, and water pushes in through cracks, window wells, and the joint where the floor meets the wall. In summer, short, intense thunderstorms can overwhelm gutters, downspouts, and sump pumps in minutes. Canyon-adjacent neighborhoods and mountain towns like Heber City, Midway, and Park City see fast-changing runoff during melt season.

Whatever the cause, the order of work is the same: make it safe, get the water out, remove what cannot be dried, dry the structure, and rebuild.

How We Clean Up a Flooded Basement

1. Make It Safe

Power to the affected area is shut off and hazards are identified before anyone works in standing water.

2. Remove the Water

Standing water is extracted so drying can start, and the flooded area is documented with photos.

3. Remove Damaged Materials

Saturated carpet pad, insulation, and drywall that wicked water are removed where needed, and we document what was removed and why.

4. Dry, Sanitize, Rebuild

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings reach a dry standard, affected surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, then repairs begin.

Flood Water vs. Roof Leaks vs. Sewage

Where the water came from matters for safety, for what can be saved, and for how the damage is documented. We separate outside floodwater and groundwater from roof-origin leaks and plumbing failures in our notes. If a roof leak is part of the problem, we can repair it; see water damage restoration. We do not clean up sewage backups. If a sewer or septic backup is involved, we will tell you so you can call a sewage cleanup specialist.

After the Water Is Out

Wet materials left in place are the main cause of lingering odor and mold after a flood. If growth has already started, our mold remediation work finds and fixes the moisture source first, then removes affected materials. For outside drainage problems that sent water toward the house, we can look at gutters and downspouts as part of the repair plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you respond to flooded basements 24/7?

Yes. Our restoration team answers water and flood emergencies 24/7 at 435-292-8802. If your basement is actively taking on water, call right away.

Is it safe to go into a flooded basement?

Not until the power to that area is off. Standing water and live electrical outlets, appliances, or extension cords are a serious shock hazard. If you cannot safely reach the breaker panel, stay out and call your utility or us. Also avoid contact with floodwater that may be contaminated.

Why does my basement flood in spring?

In much of northern Utah, snowmelt and spring runoff saturate the soil around foundations. Water then finds its way in through foundation cracks, window wells, and floor joints, or overwhelms a sump pump. Heavy summer thunderstorms can do the same thing quickly.

What gets removed after a basement flood?

It depends on how long materials were wet and what kind of water was involved. Wet carpet pad, saturated insulation, and drywall that wicked water are commonly removed so the space can dry properly. We document what is removed and why before it goes.

Do you clean up sewage backups?

No. Sewage backup cleanup is outside our scope. If a sewer or septic backup is involved, we will tell you so you can bring in a sewage cleanup specialist.

Can you help with insurance after a flood?

We document conditions with photos, moisture readings, and a written scope you can share with your insurer. Coverage decisions stay between you and your insurance company; we document and restore, we do not adjust claims. Flood coverage is often separate from a standard homeowners policy, so check your policy for what applies.
